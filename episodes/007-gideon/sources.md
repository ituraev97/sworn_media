## Primary documents
- **[OP] Gideon v. Wainwright, 372 U.S. 335 (1963)** — United States Reports vol. 372 (scan): https://archive.org/details/sim_united-states-supreme-court-cases-adjudged_february-18-april-26-1963_372 (leaves 398–414; text via _djvu.txt). Syllabus: No. 155, argued Jan 15, 1963, decided Mar 18, 1963; held right to counsel fundamental; Betts overruled; Fortas "by appointment of the Court"; Bruce R. Jacob, Assistant AG of Florida, argued for respondent; J. Lee Rankin for ACLU et al.; George D. Mentz for Alabama (with North Carolina); 22 states brief (McCormack, Mondale…). Opinion of Black, J.: colloquy ("I am sorry, but I cannot appoint Counsel…" / "The United States Supreme Court says I am entitled to be represented by Counsel."); "about as well as could be expected from a layman"; opening statement, cross-examined, presented witnesses, declined to testify, short argument; jury verdict guilty, five years in state prison; Florida Supreme Court denied relief "without an opinion"; question "Should this Court's holding in Betts v. Brady, 316 U.S. 455, be reconsidered?"; Betts indicted for robbery in Maryland, asked for counsel, told not the practice except murder and rape cases, found guilty by judge, eight years; Betts "decided by a divided Court"; Betts quote "appointment of counsel is not a fundamental right, essential to a fair trial" (316 U.S. at 471) and "totality of facts"; "so nearly indistinguishable"; "Upon full reconsideration we conclude that Betts v. Brady should be overruled."; "any person haled into court, who is too poor to hire a lawyer, cannot be assured a fair trial unless counsel is provided for him. This seems to us to be an obvious truth."; "lawyers in criminal courts are necessities, not luxuries"; Powell v. Alabama (Sutherland) quote "lacks both the skill and knowledge adequately to prepare his defense, even though he have a perfect one. He requires the guiding hand of counsel at every step in the proceedings against him."; "Florida, supported by two other States, has asked that Betts v. Brady be left intact. Twenty-two States, as friends of the Court, argue that Betts was 'an anachronism when handed down' and that it should now be overruled. We agree."; "Reversed." Harlan, J., concurring: "I agree that Betts v. Brady should be overruled, but consider it entitled to a more respectful burial than has been accorded". Clark, J., concurring in result: "the Constitution makes no distinction between capital and noncapital cases."
- **[REC] Transcript of Record, Gideon v. Cochran, No. 155, Oct. Term 1962** (U.S. Government Printing Office, 1962): https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385001_0301 ("1. No Transcript of Record.pdf"). Contains:
  - Information, Circuit Court of Bay County, filed June 19, 1961, J. Frank Adams, State Attorney: Gideon "on the 3rd day of June, A.D. 1961 … did unlawfully and feloniously break and enter a building of another, to-wit, The Bay Harbor Poolroom, property of Ira Strickland, Jr., lessee, with intent to commit a misdemeanor within said building, to-wit, petit larceny" (shown).
  - Minute book Aug 4, 1961: jury of six (Hugh W. White Jr., … C. J. Sampley, foreman); "We, the jury, find the defendant, Clarence Earl Gideon, guilty as charged. So say we all." (shown)
  - Minute book Aug 25, 1961: sentence "committed to the State Prison of the State of Florida for a period of Five Years." Commitment: conviction Aug 4, 1961; sentence Aug 25, 1961; five years. (shown)
  - Reporter's transcript of trial, Aug 4, 1961 (QUOTED, not shown): Judge Robert L. McCrary Jr.; "The Defendant: I am not ready, your Honor." … "I have no Counsel."; "Your Honor, I said: I request this Court to appoint Counsel to represent me in this trial."; judge to reporter: "Let the record show that the Defendant has asked the Court to appoint Counsel…"; "Are you now ready to go to trial?" "Yes, sir."; six men called as prospective jurors; "Gentlemen, since this Defendant is not represented by Counsel, I want to ask you some questions on his behalf" … "Will you give him the same fair trial and consideration … that you would if he were represented by Counsel?" "Yes, sir."; Henry Cook testimony: in or near the Bay Harbor Pool Room June 3, 1961 "Approximately five-thirty, A.M."; "I saw him in the Pool Room"; "He was standing up by the Cigarette Machine"; came out "At the back door"; "Went down to the Drug Store and called a Cab"; "I saw that he had some Wine"; owner/witnesses: "A Cigarette Machine and 'Juke' Box had been broken" into; "A small amount of Beer and some Wine".
  - Gideon's petition for habeas corpus to the Florida Supreme Court, sworn Oct 9, 1961, filed Oct 11, 1961 (QUOTED, not shown): "On the 3rd day of June, A.D. 1961 I was arrested and charged…"; "I was without funds and without an attorney. I asked this court to appoint me an attorney but they denied me that right."
  - Florida Supreme Court, July Term 1961, Order Denying Petition for Writ of Habeas Corpus, October 30, 1961 (shown).
  - U.S. Supreme Court, No. 890 Misc., Order granting motion for leave to proceed in forma pauperis and petition for writ of certiorari, June 4, 1962; "Should this Court's holding in Betts v. Brady, 316 U.S. 455, be reconsidered?"; Frankfurter took no part (shown). Cover: "Petition for certiorari filed January 8, 1962 · Certiorari granted June 4, 1962" (shown).
- **[FLB] Brief for the Respondent (Richard W. Ervin, Attorney General of Florida; Bruce R. Jacob; A. G. Spicola Jr.)**, same archive.org item ("7. Brief for the Respondent.pdf"), Supreme Court library stamp filed Jan 2, 1963. Statement of the case: Oct 11, 1961 petition by "an inmate of the Florida State Prison at Raiford" (p. 1). Summary p. 10: "A decision reversing the present case, if retroactive, will allow over 5,000 hardened criminals in Florida to be set free." pp. 55–56: survey by the Division of Corrections — as of June 30, 1962, 8,000 prisoners; 4,065 pleas of guilty with no counsel; 1,504 guilty with counsel; 477 not guilty without counsel; 975 not guilty with counsel; 979 unknown; "approximately 65% of those whose records were available were not represented by counsel"; "as many as 5,093 hardened criminals may be eligible to be released in one mass exodus in Florida alone"; asks that Betts be adhered to. (shown — Florida state government work)
- **[FPB] Brief for the Petitioner**, same item ("3. Brief for the Petitioner.pdf") — signature block: Arnold, Fortas & Porter, 1229 19th Street N.W., Washington, D.C.; dated Nov 21, 1962 (not shown; used only for "a Washington lawyer").
