Gideon v. Wainwright explained from the court record: in 1961 Clarence Earl Gideon, too poor to hire a lawyer, asked a Florida judge to appoint one — and the judge said no. Built from the Supreme Court's opinion, the printed record of the case (the charge, the verdict, the five-year sentence, the Florida Supreme Court's order and the Court's own question about Betts v. Brady), Florida's brief warning that "over 5,000 hardened criminals" could go free, and the account of Florida's own lawyer, this is how the right to counsel for poor defendants was decided — and what happened at Gideon's second trial.
