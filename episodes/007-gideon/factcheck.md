## FIX details (exact replacements)

**L3**: The claim holds up under Betts. In 1961 a state had to appoint counsel in non-capital cases only under "special circumstances", and the Gideon Court itself said Betts "if left standing would require us to reject Gideon's claim" (372 U.S. at 339). But a flat "He was wrong" reads as a judgment on the merits. Pin it to the time.
- say: `At the time, he was wrong. The Supreme Court had never said that, not for a case like his. Nineteen months later, it did.`
- show: `At the time, he was wrong. The Supreme Court had never said that, not for a case like his. 19 months later, it did.`
- scene.lines: `["At the time,|he was wrong.", "Then the Court|agreed."]`

**L7**: The formal charge was filed June 19 (L8). "Arrested and charged that same day" is Gideon's own wording in his petition, but next to L8 it reads as a contradiction. Jacob supports the arrest "later that morning".
- say: `That same day, Clarence Earl Gideon was arrested. He was fifty years old, with an eighth-grade education and a long criminal record, according to Bruce Jacob, the lawyer who would later argue against him.`
- show: `That same day, Clarence Earl Gideon was arrested. He was 50 years old, with an eighth-grade education and a long criminal record, according to Bruce Jacob, the lawyer who would later argue against him.`
- scene.src: `Bruce R. Jacob, “The Gideon Trials,” 99 Iowa Law Review 2059 (2014)`

**L9**: "up to five years" comes from Jacob, not from the opinion shown on screen.
- scene.src: `Gideon v. Wainwright, 372 U.S. 335 (Mar 18, 1963) · Bruce R. Jacob, “The Gideon Trials,” 99 Iowa Law Review 2059 (2014)`

**L22**: "He wrote it himself" goes further than the opinion's "apparently prepared by petitioner himself".
- say: `From the state prison at Raiford, Gideon petitioned the Florida Supreme Court for a writ of habeas corpus. He had apparently prepared it himself, the Supreme Court later noted.`
- scene.lines: `["No lawyer.", "His own|petition."]`
- scene.src: `Gideon's petition for habeas corpus, Fla. Sup. Ct., Oct 1961 (in the Transcript of Record) · Brief for the Respondent, Attorney General of Florida, No. 155 (filed Jan 1963) · Gideon v. Wainwright, 372 U.S. 335 (Mar 18, 1963)`

**L27**: Use the opinion's facts.
- say: `The case was Betts versus Brady. Smith Betts was indicted for robbery in a Maryland state court. He told the judge he had no money for a lawyer and asked for one. He was told that county appointed lawyers for poor defendants only in murder and rape cases.`
- show: `The case was Betts v. Brady. Smith Betts was indicted for robbery in a Maryland state court. He told the judge he had no money for a lawyer and asked for one. He was told that county appointed lawyers for poor defendants only in murder and rape cases.`

**L49**: "What nearly everyone already believed" overstates "widespread belief".
- say: `Governments hire lawyers to prosecute. Defendants who have money hire lawyers to defend. Those facts, the Court said, are the strongest indications of a widespread belief: lawyers in criminal courts are necessities, not luxuries.`

**L57**: "Cook admitted he had withheld details of his criminal record" is Encyclopedia.com's framing. Jacob, citing the retrial transcript, gives what was actually said. At the first trial Cook denied any felony conviction. At the retrial he said "I stoled a car one time and got put on probation for it" and agreed he had denied it before. Jacob adds that the offense was a juvenile matter, so the first answer was technically accurate. For accuracy and fairness to Cook:
- say: `The retrial took place on August fifth, nineteen sixty-three. Henry Cook testified again. At the first trial, he had denied ever being convicted of a felony. Now he said he had once stolen a car and been put on probation. Turner used that prior inconsistent statement to impeach and discredit him, Jacob later wrote, though Jacob noted the earlier offense was a juvenile matter.`
- show: `The retrial took place on August 5, 1963. Henry Cook testified again. At the first trial, he had denied ever being convicted of a felony. Now he said he had once stolen a car and been put on probation. Turner used that prior inconsistent statement to "impeach and discredit" him, Jacob later wrote, though Jacob noted the earlier offense was a juvenile matter.`
- scene.src: `Bruce R. Jacob, “The Gideon Trials,” 99 Iowa Law Review 2059 (2014)`

**L59**: "Others could be tried again" has no source. (Florida's brief said "some of them may be re-tried", but that was a prediction made before the ruling.) Remove it.
- say: `And Florida's warning? Bruce Jacob, the lawyer who had argued it, later wrote that approximately twelve hundred prisoners in Florida were set free as a result of Gideon, because witnesses had died or moved away.`
- show: `And Florida's warning? Bruce Jacob, the lawyer who had argued it, later wrote that "approximately 1200 prisoners in Florida were set free as a result of Gideon," because witnesses had died or moved away.`

**s2-0**: Put back the brief's condition.
- say: `Over five thousand hardened criminals in Florida, set free, if the ruling reached old cases. That was Florida's warning to the Supreme Court.`
- show: `"Over 5,000 hardened criminals in Florida," set free, if the ruling reached old cases. That was Florida's warning to the Supreme Court.`
