- **[CA5] United States v. Skilling, No. 06-20885 (5th Cir.), Pet. App. 1a–135a, "Filed: January 9, 2009" (SG brief: judgment entered Jan 6, 2009)** — [p55] caption, Prado J.; "A jury convicted former Enron Corporation CEO Jeffrey K. Skilling … for conspiracy, securities fraud, making false representations to auditors, and insider trading." [p56] founded Wholesale 1990; President & COO 1997; CEO Feb 2001; resigned Aug 14, 2001; "About four months after Skilling's departure, Enron crashed into sudden bankruptcy."; Enron Task Force; Lay "Enron's CEO until Skilling took over (and again after his abrupt exit)"; Fastow CFO. Skilling "reportedly told Ken Rice ('Rice'), EBS's CEO, that if investors perceived Enron as a trading company, its stock would 'get whacked.'" California utilities "owed Enron substantial fees … stopped paying". [p59–60] "Enron invested more than $1 billion in EBS"; Rice "likened the monetizations to 'one more hit of crack cocaine on these earnings.'"; "Ultimately, Enron lost the entire $1 billion that it had initially invested in EBS." [p61] Jan 22, 2001 call quote "the situation in California … had little impact on fourth quarter results. Let me repeat that. For Enron, the situation in California had little impact on fourth quarter results."; "Skilling allegedly knew that the California utilities likely could not pay … and that Enron might have to write off a loss of hundreds of millions of dollars."; three days later annual analyst conference: "sustainable high earnings power"; Wholesale "not a trading business. We are a logistics company." [p62–64] consensus estimate; Skilling "particularly committed to hitting or beating the consensus estimate"; Q2 2000 consensus 32¢, draft met it, Skilling wanted 34¢, "told Wholesale to increase its earnings by $7 million, and then by an additional $7 million … adding $14 million from a reserve account that it had set aside to cover potential liabilities." LJM: Fastow LJM's general partner; Cuiaba (Brazilian power plant) 1999; Skilling "allegedly replied, 'Don't worry. [I]ll make sure that you're all right on the project. You won't lose any money.'"; "That oral understanding did not appear in the deal write-up, and the accountants treated it as a real sale"; Enron bought back "paying full value (notwithstanding depreciation) plus 13%". [p188–189] retirement plans not "financial institutions"; "The court therefore erred in applying this enhancement."; "we AFFIRM Skilling's convictions in all respects. We VACATE Skilling's sentence and REMAND for resentencing." (shown)

- **[SG] Brief for the United States, No. 08-1394 (Elena Kagan, Solicitor General), merits stage 2010** — [p2706] cover (shown). [p2707] convictions: Count 1 conspiracy; 12 securities fraud (2, 14, 16-20, 22-26); 5 false representations to auditors (31-32, 34-36); 1 insider trading (Count 51); "Petitioner was the president, chief operating officer, and, for several months in 2001, the chief executive officer"; Enron formed by merger of two natural gas pipeline companies in 1985; steady growth through the 1990s due largely to energy trading; early 1999 stock about 25× earnings; "compensation was tied directly to the value of the company's stock, wished to increase the share price even further"; "as he told his managers, '[t]here ain't no more 'E' in the earnings.'" (shown). [p2708] EES (energy at retail) and EBS (telecommunications); "EBS lost money in every quarter that it existed"; reorganization "two days before the quarter ended"; "The sole purpose of the reorganization was to hide the losses in EES by shifting all of its money-losing components into the larger balance sheet of Enron's wholesale division"; head of EES: approval "the worst corporate conduct he had ever experienced", "I wish on my kids' lives I would have stepped up from that table and walked away."; "Enron reported first quarter earnings for EES of $40 million, when in fact the business should have recognized a $350 million loss." (shown). [p2709] "monetized" projected future revenue; March 2001 special call "great quarter"; EBS COO: "the opposite" of the truth (shown). [p2710] "beat the street"; auditor: "That is not a gray area. It's black and white."; Aug 14, 2001 abrupt resignation; "By that time, petitioner knew that Enron faced mounting problems and was suffering substantial undisclosed losses."; Sept 6, 2001 after meeting Lay, tried to sell 200,000 shares; broker: report to SEC and public unless letter that he was no longer a manager; told broker to hold; letter Sept 10, 2001; Sept 17 first day markets opened after 9/11, order to sell 500,000 shares "along with instructions that he did not want people at Enron to know about the sale"; "netted more than $15 million" (shown). [p2711] SEC testimony: "scared" by September 11, "[t]here was no other reason other than September 11th that [he] sold the stock."; "Less than three months after petitioner's trade, Enron collapsed in bankruptcy."; Feb 18, 2004 indictment of petitioner and Causey (chief accounting officer); July 7, 2004 superseding indictment adding Lay; venue motion: "inflammatory pretrial publicity and pervasive community prejudice"; Causey pled guilty (renewed motion Jan 2006); 14-page questionnaire to 400 prospective jurors, 283 responses (shown).

## Other primary
- **[DOJ06] DOJ press release #06-723, Oct 23, 2006** (shell download): https://www.justice.gov/archive/opa/pr/2006/October/06_crm_723.html — 24 years and four months; forfeit approximately $45 million "to be applied towards restitution for the victims"; convicted May 25, 2006 of 12 counts securities fraud, one insider trading, conspiracy and five false statements to auditors; Lay convicted "after 56 days of trial and jury deliberations"; stock "from approximately $30 per share in early 1998 to over $80 per share in January 2001"; bankruptcy Dec 2001 "making its stock virtually worthless"; "thousands of workers lost their jobs"; Fisher: "Jeffrey Skilling will now spend more than 24 years in prison for committing one of the largest frauds in the history of corporate America."; "Kenneth Lay died on July 5, 2006, and his conviction was voided by a federal judge earlier this month." - **[DOJ13] DOJ press release, Jun 21, 2013** (WebFetch): https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/former-enron-ceo-jeffrey-skilling-resentenced-168-months-fraud-conspiracy-charges — resentenced to 168 months; forfeit about $42 million for restitution to Enron fraud victims; original 292 months Oct 2006; Jan 2009 Fifth Circuit affirmed convictions, vacated sentence (pension-plan enhancement); May 2013 agreement: jointly recommend 168–210 months, not contest forfeiture and restitution order, waive appeals.
- **[SC] Skilling v. United States, 561 U.S. 358 (2010)** (Justia, WebFetch): https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/561/358/ — "In 2001, Enron Corporation, then the seventh highest-revenue-grossing company in America, crashed into bankruptcy."; "We therefore hold that §1346 covers only bribery and kickback schemes."; "Because Skilling's alleged misconduct entailed no bribe or kickback, it does not fall within §1346's proscription."; "Finally, and of prime significance, Skilling's jury acquitted him of nine insider-trading counts."; "We therefore affirm in part and vacate in part." Opinion by Ginsburg, J.; decided June 24, 2010; argued March 1, 2010 (Wikipedia). Holding that pretrial publicity and community prejudice did not prevent a fair trial (Wikipedia summary + opinion).
